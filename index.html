Ofe Nsala and Pounded Yam

A rich, light Igbo white soup paired with smooth pounded yam.

About This Recipe

Nsala Soup (Ofe Nsala) also called White Soup is a traditional Igbo delicacy from Eastern Nigeria. Unlike most Nigerian soups, it made without palm oil, giving it a clean, light broth with a deeply aromatic flavour It also contains small pieces of yam or cocoyam with utazi leaves. It is similar to the Efik Afia Efere and almost always paired with pounded yam - one of Nigeria's most beloved swallows.

Recipe Details

Prep Time: 25 minutes

Cook Time: 60 minutes

Total Time: 1 hour 25 minutes

Servings: 5 people

Difficulty: Medium

Cuisine: Nigerian (Igbo)


How It Looks

Nsala Soup - light, aromatic, and full of flavour.


Nsala Soup Ingredients

  • 1.3KG / 3lb Chicken/1 kg of catfish (fresh or dried), cleaned and cut
  • 4 to 5 small slices of raw yam (for thickening the soup)
  • 1 teaspoon of ground ehuru (African nutmeg)
  • Dried or smoked fish (two medium sizes)
  • 2 seasoning cubes (Maggi or Knorr)
  • 1 handful of utazi leaves, shredded
  • 2 tablespoons of ground crayfish
  • 2 cups of water or fish stock
  • 1 cup sliced Uziza leaves
  • 1 teaspoon Uziza seeds
  • One stalk of Aidan seed
  • Snails (Optional)
  • Ogili(Optional)
  • Fresh Pepper
  • Salt to taste.

Instructions - Nsala Soup

  1. Prepare the fish: Clean the catfish thoroughly with hot water and a little salt to remove slime. Cut into large chunks and set aside.
  2. Cook the yam thickener: Peel and boil 3 small pieces of raw yam until very soft. Pound or blend them into a smooth paste; this replaces palm oil and thickens the soup naturally.
  3. Start the broth: Place the catfish (and stockfish if using) in a pot. Add water or fish stock, pepper, seasoning cubes, ground ehuru, and uziza seeds. Cook on medium heat for 15 minutes.
  4. Add the yam paste: Drop spoonfuls of the pounded yam paste into the simmering broth. Stir gently and let it dissolve to thicken the soup.
  5. Add crayfish and utazi: Stir in the ground crayfish. Add the shredded utazi leaves last; do not overcook them or they become bitter.
  6. Taste and finish: Adjust salt and seasoning. Simmer for 5 more minutes and remove from heat. Your Nsala Soup is ready!

Ingredients - Pounded Yam

  • 1 large tuber of white yam
  • Water for boiling
  • A pinch of salt (optional)

Instructions - Pounded Yam

  1. Peel and cut: Peel the yam tuber and cut into medium chunks. Rinse well under clean water.
  2. Boil the yam: Place the yam chunks in a pot of water. Boil for about 25 to 30 minutes until they are very soft and a fork passes through them easily.
  3. Drain the water: Pour out most of the water but keep a small cup aside as you may need it while pounding.
  4. Pound the yam: Transfer the hot yam into a mortar. Pound with a pestle, turning and folding as you go. Add small splashes of the reserved water if it feels too stiff.
  5. Smooth and serve: Keep pounding until the yam is smooth, stretchy, and lump-free. Mould into balls and serve immediately alongside the Nsala Soup.

Tips & Notes

  • The soup should be light and slightly thick, not too watery, not too heavy. The yam paste controls the thickness.
  • If you have no mortar? You can use a hand mixer or food processor to pound the yam - it still comes out smooth.
  • Add utazi leaves last and only simmer briefly, leaving it for too long makes the soup bitter.
  • Fresh catfish gives the best flavour, but you can mix fresh and dried fish for a richer broth.
  • Nsala Soup tastes even better reheated the next day as the spices deepen.
